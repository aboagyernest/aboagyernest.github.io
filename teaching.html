Teaching

I teach the quantitative core of risk management and actuarial science: probability, financial mathematics, data analysis, and the models that turn them into insurance and investment decisions. Before academia I spent several years as a practicing actuary, and that experience runs through my teaching: students learn a technique best when they see the decision it serves.

Approach

  • Worked problems, then student problems. I solve a representative problem in full, narrating each choice, then give students a closely related problem to attempt in class before moving on.
  • Applications from practice. Examples come from pension valuation, life pricing and commercial lines pricing, and from my research on flood and crop insurance.
  • Individual attention. I learn names, check in with each student during problem sessions, and hold regular and on-demand office hours.
  • Professional standards. I redeveloped Georgia State's AS 3230 Financial Mathematics so that it was approved for the Society of Actuaries' University Earned Credit program, and now teach the corresponding course, MA 37300, at Purdue.
